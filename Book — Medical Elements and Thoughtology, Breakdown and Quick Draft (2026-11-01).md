## Part II. The book — proposed structure

**Working title (his to approve, not imposed):** *Medical Elements — How Thoughtology Puts the Pieces Together*
**Alternative titles, if he prefers:** *The Elements of Medicine* / *Thoughtology: A Medical Elements Book* / *Shape, Color, and Size: A Book of Medical Elements*

**Front matter:** The Classroom Method (filed Oct 31, 2026) — theories may be intentionally wrong so the class can test them; a "breaks" verdict is the class working, not a failure. Dual register throughout: every chapter carries a 13th-grade treatment and a "Simply put" telling. Old-with-new: his original wording stays beside every rewrite. Shiro column: PENDING in every table.

- **Chapter 1 — Elements.** The table above, expanded: each element defined, each with its "Simply put" line and its GAPs. The book's periodic table, his way.
- **Chapter 2 — Shape: The Lincoln Log Body.** Shape-sorter model, receptor fit, antibody fit, cross-reactivity as the sloppy key. (Lincoln log theory.)
- **Chapter 3 — Color: The Spectrum.** False color, family labels, serogroups as colors. California serogroup as the worked example — 18 members, one color, many shades. (Lincoln log P2 + serogroup file.)
- **Chapter 4 — Size: Small and Large.** The size ladder (20 nm to 300 nm), the crowded 100 nm band, imbalance as description, missing-virus vs. missing-antibody split, IVIG reframe. (Size-imbalance theory.)
- **Chapter 5 — Fingers: Spikes That Interlock.** Spike–receptor lock, cell–cell fusion, syncytin as the domesticated spike, cadherins vs. spikes — three interlocks, three jobs. (Spikes theory.)
- **Chapter 6 — Clearing: The Relay.** Finger grabs cell, cell bursts, debris enters mucus/blood/gut, phagocytes/liver/kidney/cilia/bowel clear it. Why viruses don't eat, and why the relay still holds. (Fingers/clearing theory.)
- **Chapter 7 — Overburden and Fever.** Burden as clearance traffic vs. severity, trajectory (slope) as the decider, fever's arrow corrected (virus triggers fever; fever fights virus), leaving through cleansing, latency as the exception. (Overburden/fever theory.)
- **Chapter 8 — The Lung Exit: Sputum.** Sputum as truck and cargo, mucus composition, cough as cleansing, viruses in sputum without sputum being virus. (Sputum/stool theory, lung half.)
- **Chapter 9 — The Bowel Exit: Stool and Colonization.** Stool as medical element, bacteria/fungi/viruses together, oils and acids kept separate, castor oil history, chunk vs. colonization — colonization wins. (Sputum/stool theory, bowel half.)
- **Chapter 10 — The Fungus Question.** The fungal-only theory in full, tested, rewritten: bacteria and fungi both carry viruses, differently (arming/lethal vs. latent/modulating). Meningitis, pneumococcus, tuberculosis as the three worked rows. (Fungus-only theory.)
- **Chapter 11 — Thoughtology: The Method.** How he thinks: shape + color + size + showing; test three ways; split compound dictations; combined statements; classroom sparring; 51% bar; six tracks; dual register. His Oct 17 §179 method ("thought experiments go back in time… fill in gaps… reassess old medicine") as the chapter's spine.
- **Chapter 12 — What Stays Open.** The GAP ledger for the whole book: Shiro definition, Salo vs. Obaid, "fingers" in any remaining use, the do-not-modify list, the missing-virus causal claim, the chunk (retired, with reason). Nothing invented to close a gap.
- **Appendix — The Tables.** California serogroup member table; size ladder; sputum vs. stool composition; bacterium–fungus–virus table for meningitis/pneumococcus/TB; immunoglobulin vs. virus-gap test pair.

## Part III. Quick-draft opening — Chapter 1, first page (his method, in his register)

> Medicine, as I have learned to think it, is not one big thing. It is elements, the way a Lincoln log set is logs, and a child's shape-sorter is circles and triangles and blocks. Each element goes in one place — and each element has a color, a shape, and a size, and if you sort by only one of those you will miss the other two.
>
> A virus is an element. A fungus is an element. A bacterium is an element. Yeast is its own element, and mold is its own element, and I will not let anyone merge them for me. Sputum is an element. Stool is an element — I call it a medical element, because old doctors read it and new doctors should too. A spike is an element. An antibody is an element. An oil is an element. An acid is an element, and there are three acids, not one, and I will keep them apart.
>
> Thoughtology is what you do with the elements. You put them together, you test them three ways, you let them break where they break, and you keep the broken piece beside the fixed piece, old with new, so the reader can see what held and what did not. That is this book.

**Simply put:** This book sorts medicine into pieces you can hold in your hand — germs, spit, stool, spikes, antibodies — and then shows how thinking with those pieces, tested honestly, makes sense of sickness.
